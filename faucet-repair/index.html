Faucet or supply line leak emergency: stop it fast

Most sink and tub emergencies come down to a failed supply line, a corroded shutoff or a faucet body that has split. The first fix is always to cut the water.

Kitchen and bath

Dripping, loose and low-flow faucets

A drip comes from a worn cartridge, washer or seat. A loose handle means a stripped set screw or worn stem, and low flow often comes from a clogged aerator or a partly closed stop. Kitchen faucets add sprayers and diverters that fail on their own schedule.

In many cases a cartridge swap is cheaper than a new faucet. If the faucet is old, pitted or no longer carries parts, replacement is the better value.

Tubs and showers

Tub faucets, shower valves and handles that will not shut off

A tub spout that drips or a shower valve that will not fully close is wasting hot water as well as cold. The cause is usually a worn cartridge or a stem seal, and repair means opening the wall access or removing the trim.

Older homes may have a valve with no shutoff, which means closing the whole house to work on it. We often add stops during the repair so future jobs are simpler.

Shutoff valves

Replacing stuck angle stops and old supply lines

Braided supply lines last years, not decades, and rubber washers inside old stops harden. A line that has been under pressure for a long time can burst without warning, often while you are away.

We replace both when they show corrosion or have not moved in years. Where the leak is behind a wall or in a place you cannot see, leak detection confirms the source before we cut.

Local

Hard water, scale and Sanford faucets

The City of Sanford's annual water quality report covers chlorine, fluoride and lead and does not state a hardness figure. Test your tap or ask the utility, because mineral buildup clogs aerators and shortens cartridge life where water is hard.

If faucets and fixtures all show scale, a water softener or filtration system may be worth discussing after you know your numbers.

Rules and references

Water use guidelines for replacement faucets

If a faucet is past repair, the EPA WaterSense label marks bathroom faucets that use no more than 1.5 gallons per minute, according to the program's published specification. A labeled model cuts water use without a loss in everyday performance.

Supply lines and shutoff valves under the sink should match the fixture instructions, so check the manufacturer guidelines before you buy parts.
