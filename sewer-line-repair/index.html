Sewer emergency

Sewer backup emergency: protect the house first

When the line is broken or blocked beyond a cleanout, waste has nowhere to go but your drains. Quick steps limit what the water touches.

  1. 1
    Stop all water use. Every flush and wash adds to the backup.
  2. 2
    Close off the area. Keep children and pets out and wear gloves if you must handle anything.
  3. 3
    Do not use caustic cleaners. They harm pipes and create a hazard for the plumber.
  4. 4
    Call and ask about help. Describe where the backup is showing, because that guides the first visit.
Diagnosis

Blockage or broken pipe: a camera tells the difference

Not every sewer backup needs a repair. A blockage clears with a cable or hydro jetting, and a broken, bellied or root-invaded line will clog again until it is fixed.

A camera on a flexible cable shows which one you have. We use sewer camera inspection to find offsets, cracks, collapses and root intrusions before recommending a repair, so the scope matches the damage.

Repair options

Spot repair, lining and full replacement

A spot repair replaces one damaged section and works where the rest of the line is sound. Trenchless lining feeds a resin-saturated liner through the pipe and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one, which avoids digging up lawns and driveways. Full replacement uses a trench or pipe bursting when the line has failed along its length.

Which one fits depends on the pipe material, how much is damaged and what sits above the line. Trees, driveways and patios all change the answer, and we explain trade-offs honestly.

Cast iron and clay

Cast iron, clay and older pipe materials

Older homes and downtown commercial buildings can still have cast iron or clay drain lines. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out and eventually flakes or cracks, and clay pipe has joints that roots find easily.

Houses from the early twentieth century near Historic Downtown were rebuilt and extended many times, so the pipe on paper may not match what is underground. That is why a camera is worth doing first.

Local

Sewer lines in Sanford and Seminole County

Lateral lines in Seminole County connect to either a city sewer main or a septic system, and who owns what changes from one street to the next. Before any dig, we confirm where your property line and the utility connection are, and we call for utility locates as the state requires before excavation.

Sandy Central Florida soil and a high water table in low areas can make trenches collapse and fill with groundwater, which affects the method and schedule. If you also notice slow drains, see drain cleaning for a quick first check.

Rules and references

Permits and inspections for sewer repair

According to code guidelines, sewer repair is regulated plumbing work under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and many jurisdictions require a permit for excavation and replacement. Ask your building department what applies before digging starts.

Call 811 before any dig. Florida law requires a locate request so buried utilities are marked, and a plumber should confirm the marks before the trench opens.
