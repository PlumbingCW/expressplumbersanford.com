An emergency plumber on the phone at 2 a.m.

Plumbing emergencies start at inconvenient hours, usually with water moving somewhere it should not. Call the phone line and describe the problem, and the person who answers will ask the right questions.

While you arrange help

What to do in the first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

The first few minutes decide how much damage a plumbing emergency does. These steps work for most problems and cost nothing.

  1. 1
    Shut off the water. Turn the main water valve clockwise for any leak. It is near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. 2
    Cut the power to wet areas. Use the breaker, not a wall switch, and stay out of standing water near outlets.
  3. 3
    Stop using drains. For a backup, no flushing, laundry or dishes until a plumber has seen the line.
  4. 4
    Turn off the fuel or breaker for a water heater. Close the cold water valve above the tank too.
  5. 5
    Call. Tell us what you see, where the water is and what you have already shut off.

Photograph the damage before you clean up, because insurance adjusters like to see it. A burst pipe or sewer backup can also be covered under a homeowners policy, so ask your agent what yours includes.

Local knowledge

Sanford plumbing, from First Street to the I-4 corridor

Downtown Sanford was rebuilt in brick after a September 1887 fire on First Street, according to the City of Sanford. Storefronts and homes around Historic Downtown have been renovated for well over a century, so old and new pipe often meet inside the same wall, and that is why we look before we cut.

The City of Sanford's annual water quality report says its drinking water comes from seventeen groundwater wells in the Floridan Aquifer and is disinfected with chlorine. The report does not publish a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask the utility if scale is building on faucets and heaters.

Most homes in Seminole County are built on concrete slabs, which means supply lines often run under the floor. A hot spot on a tile floor or a meter that moves with everything off is worth a call to leak detection before the damage spreads.

Newer subdivisions along SR 46, SR 417 and US 17-92 add their own problems, from builder-grade fixtures to debris left in drains. Near Lake Monroe and the RiverWalk, mature oaks send roots toward drain lines, and a repeat clog is often the first sign.
