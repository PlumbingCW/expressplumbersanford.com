Sewer Camera Inspection in Sanford, FL

A sewer camera inspection shows what is inside the pipe, so repairs are based on pictures instead of guesses. We schedule inspections in Sanford for buyers, homeowners with repeat clogs and anyone who wants to know the condition of an older line.

Call (407) 964-8940
What it is

A camera on a flexible cable, pushed through your line

A sewer inspection feeds a waterproof camera head through the cleanout and along the drain line while a monitor shows the live view. A locator in the head lets us trace the pipe above ground so we know exactly where a problem sits and how deep it is.

You see the same picture we do. We note offsets, cracks, low spots where water pools, root intrusions, scale and any collapse, and we explain what each one means.

When to book

Good reasons to inspect a sewer line

The most common reasons are repeated backups, slow drains that cabling does not cure, a home purchase where the sewer line was not examined, a house with trees close to the pipe and an older line you want to understand before a renovation. It is also the smart first step before any dig.

If an emergency backup is the reason, our sewer line repair page covers what happens next. For a line that just needs clearing, a sewer line cleaning visit may be enough.

Before buying

Home buyers: why inspect the line before closing

A general home inspection does not include the underground drain line. Older houses near Historic Downtown and Goldsboro are likelier to have original pipe, and any buyer of a house with mature trees should know what the line looks like.

A recorded inspection gives you leverage in a negotiation and a baseline to compare against later. Ask for the video file at the end of the visit.

Rules and references

Reading a camera report

A good inspection report shows the footage, names the pipe material and notes the distance from the cleanout to each defect. Ask for a copy of the video so a second plumber can review it.

Pipe grades and sizing in Florida follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, so a line that holds water or sags may be below the standard the code expects.
