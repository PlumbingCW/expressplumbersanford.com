Planning

Before you buy: size, fuel and location

Start with how your household uses hot water. Count bathrooms, note whether two showers often run at once, and check whether the dishwasher and washer share the same heater. That picture matters more than a model number.

Then look at where the heater lives. A garage corner, an interior closet and an attic platform each carry different clearance, drain and access needs, and moving a heater is usually a larger job than swapping it in place.

Types

Tank, heat pump hybrid and tankless

Standard tanks are the simplest and the most familiar. Heat pump hybrids use less electricity but need airspace and a condensate drain. Tankless units heat on demand and need correctly sized gas or electric supply, which is covered in more detail under tankless water heater service.

If you are replacing a failed tank rather than planning a new setup, water heater replacement focuses on the shutoff steps while you wait.

Code and safety

Relief valves, drain pans and expansion tanks

Every storage heater needs a temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge line. A drain pan with a drain line is used where a leak could damage finished space, and an expansion tank is added when the plumbing system is closed by a backflow device or pressure-reducing valve.

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing sets the base rules, and permit requirements differ by city. Ask the building department that covers your address, whether that is Sanford, Lake Mary or Longwood.
