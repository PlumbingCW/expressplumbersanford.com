Repiping replaces the supply lines in your house when patches stop making sense. We plan whole-house repipes in Sanford with a clear scope. Call and ask about help.

Signs

When patching stops working

Repeated leaks in different parts of the house, low pressure throughout, brown or rusty water and visible corrosion on exposed pipe all point to a system that is wearing out. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and narrows the bore, and older copper can develop pinhole leaks.

A single leak is a repair. A pattern of leaks is a signal. If one pipe has failed, the one next to it is often from the same batch of material and the same age.

Materials

PEX, copper and what we use

PEX is a flexible plastic pipe that bends around corners, needs fewer joints and does not corrode, and it is the usual choice for repipes. Copper is rigid and long-lived, costs more and takes more labor. Each has trade-offs in cost, access and sound.

We explain both and recommend one for your house. Pipe choice also depends on the local code and the permit requirements of your jurisdiction.

Process

What a repipe involves

A repipe starts with a walkthrough of the home to count fixtures and plan routes. Lines run through attics, crawlspaces or walls, and drywall access is planned in advance to keep patching small. The old lines are disconnected and capped, and new lines are pressure tested before the walls are closed.

Most repipes take a few days depending on the house. Water is off for part of that time, and we plan the schedule so you know when it is back.

Planning the house

Planning the routes, the shutdowns and the patching

Before any pipe is cut we walk the house with you and agree where the new lines will run. Attic routes keep most of the work out of finished walls, and where a branch must pass through a wall we choose the spot with the least finish to repair. A plan on paper avoids surprises later.

Water is shut off during parts of the work, and we schedule the longest outages for the day when you can be away. We also set up a temporary water source for the day when possible. After the new lines are tested, the old ones are capped or removed, and the house is flushed so debris leaves the system.

What to do while you arrange help

How to prepare your home for a repipe

Clear the areas around the water heater, the main shutoff and the under-sink cabinets. Move furniture and fragile items out of rooms where walls may be opened.

Plan for the water to be off for part of the day, store drinking water and tell everyone in the house. Ask in advance about patching and painting, because most crews leave finished repairs to a separate trade.

Rules and references

Permits and pipe standards for repiping

A repipe is a large change to the system, so it is normally permitted and inspected under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. Ask the building department that covers your address what applies, and expect the work to be checked against the code the city enforces.
