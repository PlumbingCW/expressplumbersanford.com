Service line break

Water service line emergency: yard flooding or no water

The service line runs from the meter to your house, usually underground. When it fails, water surfaces in the yard, pressure drops or the water goes out entirely.

  1. 1
    Close the meter valve. The shutoff is at the meter, and the utility owns the meter side.
  2. 2
    Check the street side. If water is at the meter box itself, the utility may need to be called.
  3. 3
    Mark the wet area. Keep vehicles and pets away from soft ground.
  4. 4
    Call and ask about help. Describe where the water is surfacing.
Symptoms

Signs of a failing service line

Common signs are a wet or sunken patch in the yard with no rain, low pressure throughout the house, a meter that runs with every fixture off, discolored water after a break and a hissing sound near the meter box.

Many callers first notice a high water bill. The first test is the one described under leak detection, which separates a leak on the utility side from one in the house.

Who owns what

Utility side versus owner side

The water meter marks a boundary. Utilities normally own and maintain the line up to the meter, and the property owner is responsible from the meter to the house. Rules differ, so confirm the boundary with the utility that serves your address.

If the leak is at the meter or on the street side, the utility handles it. If it is on the house side, we repair or replace the line.

Repair options

Spot repair, replacement and trenchless methods

A spot repair cuts into the line at the break and replaces that section. A full replacement runs a new line from the meter to the house, in a trench or with pipe bursting, which pulls a new pipe through the old path.

Low-pressure complaints can have other causes. A restriction, a failing pressure regulator or a partly closed valve can mimic a service line problem, and we check those first when the symptoms fit.

Service lines in Sanford, Lake Mary and Longwood

Sanford's drinking water comes from seventeen groundwater wells in the Floridan Aquifer, and the City of Sanford maintains a service line inventory as part of its lead program, according to its water quality report. Utilities in Lake Mary and Longwood keep their own records, so ask yours.

Sandy soil and sprinkler lines make digging easier in some yards and trickier in others. We call for utility locates before excavation, and a leak in a front yard near a driveway or sidewalk changes the repair method.
