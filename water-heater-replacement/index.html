A water heater that has failed outright leaves a household without hot water, so call and ask about help. If the tank is leaking, shut off the fuel or the breaker, close the cold water valve at the top and mop what you can reach so water does not run under flooring.

Failed tank

When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank that has rusted through cannot be repaired. Water spreads fast on a slab, and attic installations can drop it through a ceiling, so the shutoff steps matter more than the wait.

  1. 1
    Cut the fuel or power. Gas control to off, or the dedicated breaker.
  2. 2
    Close the cold water valve. It sits on the pipe above the tank.
  3. 3
    Open a hot tap. Opening a faucet upstairs relieves pressure and speeds draining if you must empty the tank.
  4. 4
    Call and describe the unit. Gas or electric, tank size on the label and where it sits, so the correct heater is on the truck.
Decision

Replace now or repair one more time

Replacement makes sense when the tank itself leaks, when rust shows in hot water, when the unit is well past a decade old or when repairs are stacking up. A heater that only needs an element or a thermocouple is a repair, and we will say so rather than sell a new one.

If your problem is a part, water heater repair is the cheaper path. If you are also thinking about on-demand heating, a tankless water heater changes the plumbing and venting, so compare the two before choosing.

Sizing

Choosing size and fuel for your household

Size follows the number of people and how hot water is used. A bigger family with several bathrooms runs a larger tank or a high-recovery model, and a couple in a small house is fine with less. Fuel is usually dictated by what is already connected, because changing from electric to gas means running a new line and vent.

Local homes often have the heater in a garage or closet with limited clearance, so we measure the space before ordering. Taller or wider tanks do not always fit the old spot, and relocating a heater is part of water heater installation rather than a simple swap.

What replacement includes

What a replacement visit covers

A proper replacement is more than swapping tanks. The old unit is drained, disconnected and hauled away, and new supply connections, a relief valve with a discharge line and a drain pan where one is needed are installed.

Gas units get a vent and combustion check. Electric units get a power and thermostat check. Permit requirements vary, so ask the City of Sanford building department what applies to your address, and tell us if your home is in a different jurisdiction.

Local

Replacements in Sanford homes

Sanford spans homes from early twentieth century houses near Historic Downtown to newer subdivisions along the SR 46 and I-4 corridors. Older houses may have undersized or corroded shutoff valves that should be replaced during the swap, and newer homes may need an expansion tank if a backflow device is on the line.

Ask us to check the water pressure while we are there, because high pressure can shorten the life of the new heater and every fixture on the line.

What to do while you arrange help

If your water heater is failing today

Turn off the power to an electric heater at the breaker, or set a gas heater control to pilot or off. Close the cold water inlet valve on top of the tank.

If water is pooling, attach a hose to the drain valve and run it outside, or use towels and a wet vac. Do not touch a unit that is leaking near live electrical parts.
