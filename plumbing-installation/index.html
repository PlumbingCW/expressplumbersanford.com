Plumbing Installation in Sanford, FL

Plumbing installation covers new fixtures, added bathrooms, kitchen remodels and new lines in homes and small commercial spaces in Sanford. We plan the work, rough in the pipes and connect the fixtures. Call and ask about help.

Scope

What plumbing installation covers

Typical jobs include adding a bathroom or half-bath, moving a sink or toilet during a remodel, installing a utility sink or laundry hookup, adding hose bibs or an outdoor shower, and running supply to a detached garage or accessory dwelling. Each one needs a plan for supply, drain and vent.

Replacing a single fixture is simpler than adding a new one. For a toilet swap or faucet change on its own, see toilet repair and installation and faucet repair.

Planning

Supply, drain and vent: the three systems behind every fixture

Supply brings hot and cold water in, the drain carries waste out by gravity and the vent lets air in so the drain flows without gurgling. A fixture that is placed where none of the three can reach it needs rerouting, and every added fixture must fit the code's rules for slope and vent distance.

That is why a bathroom on the far side of a slab house costs more than one stacked above an existing bathroom. We review where your lines already run before suggesting a layout.

Rough-in

Rough-in, inspection and finish

Rough-in is the stage where pipes are placed before walls close. Most jurisdictions require an inspection at this point, and the pipes are pressure or water tested. After the inspection, walls are closed and fixtures are set in the finish stage.

Permit rules vary by city, so ask the City of Sanford building department about your address or tell us if your home is in Lake Mary or Longwood. We follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing and the local requirements.

Local

Remodels in older Sanford homes

Historic homes near First Street and the Sanford Residential Historic District carry plumbing from several eras, and a remodel often exposes old pipe. We check what you have before tying new lines into it, since a new bathroom connected to weak galvanized supply lowers pressure for the whole house.

If your house has old supply lines throughout, a whole-house repipe before or during the remodel avoids opening walls twice.

Choosing fixtures

Fixtures, rough-in dimensions and what to have on site

Fixture choice changes the rough-in. A wall-hung toilet needs a carrier inside the wall, a freestanding tub needs a floor drain in a precise spot, and a vessel sink changes the faucet height. Choose or at least shortlist fixtures before the pipes are placed, because moving a drain after the wall is closed costs far more than a different faucet.

Bring the specification sheets for anything you have already bought. We check the rough-in measurements against the drawings, and we flag any fixture that will not meet code or clear the cabinet before the work starts. Delivery dates matter too, since a missing tub can hold up an inspection.
