Tub Faucet Repair in Sanford, FL: Drips, Cartridges and Valves

A tub faucet that drips or will not shut off is usually a worn cartridge, washer or seat, and the repair depends on the kind of valve behind the wall. Many fixes are simple, and some need a plumber because the valve is hidden. This guide explains the causes and the choices.

By the Sanford Express Plumbing team · Updated November 13, 2026

Call (407) 964-8940

Know your faucet type

Tub faucets come as two-handle compression valves, single-handle cartridge or ball valves and diverter spouts. Each wears in a different way, so identify yours before you buy parts.

Remove the handle cover and look at the stem. A rubber washer points to a compression valve, a plastic or brass cylinder is a cartridge, and a rotating ball inside the body is a ball valve.

Dripping spout

A drip from the spout means water is leaking past the valve seat or the cartridge seal. On compression valves a worn washer or a pitted seat is the cause. On a cartridge valve, a worn cartridge is likely.

Water that runs only when the diverter is up points to a diverter problem. A drip that is hot or cold only identifies the side to repair.

Handle will not shut off

A handle that spins freely may have a stripped stem or a worn set screw. A handle that will not fully close can have a corroded stem or a failed cartridge.

Do not force it. A broken stem can leave you without any way to shut off the tub, and you may need to close the main water valve for the repair.

Basic repair steps

Turn off the water at the valve or the main, open the faucet to drain the lines and remove the handle and trim. Pull the cartridge or stem and replace the washer, seat or cartridge with an exact match.

Reassemble, turn the water on slowly and test hot and cold. Some tub valves have no shutoff of their own, and that is often the biggest challenge.

When the valve is behind the wall

Many tub and shower valves can be reached from the front, but some need access from behind. A plumber can open a small access panel in the next room and avoid breaking the tile.

If the valve itself is corroded, replacement is the long-term fix. Our faucet repair page covers fixtures, valves and supply lines, and leak detection helps when you suspect a hidden leak in the wall.

Aerators, diverters and low flow

A tub spout does not have an aerator, but a shower head does, and a clogged one can lower flow. Mineral scale also builds in diverters and causes poor shutoff to the shower.

Soaking a shower head in vinegar clears light scale. Heavy scale means a replacement part.

Parts to buy and what to match

Bring the old cartridge or stem to the store or note the brand stamped on the valve. Tub valves are not interchangeable, and a close match that is not exact will leak.

Buy a spare O-ring set and plumber's grease while you are there. Replacing the O-rings costs little and saves a second teardown.

Preventing the next drip

Do not overtighten handles. Closing a valve with force wears the washer and seat faster than light pressure.

Run hot and cold through the tub a few times a month if the bathroom is rarely used. Minerals settle in idle valves, and an occasional flush keeps them moving.

When to replace the whole fixture

A faucet that has been repaired several times, a valve body that is cracked or pitted and trim that is discolored are reasons to replace the fixture. A new valve and trim also give you a chance to add a shutoff and a modern cartridge.

Replacement means opening the wall or working from behind, so ask for a plan and a price before the work starts.

Parts and water use guidelines

Replacement shower and tub valves should carry a marking showing they meet the standards for anti-scald protection. According to plumbing code guidelines, a tub and shower valve must limit the hot water temperature to reduce scald risk.

Local note

Hard water questions for Sanford tubs

The City of Sanford's annual water quality report covers chlorine, fluoride and lead and does not give a hardness figure. Test your tap or ask the City of Sanford Utility Department, because mineral scale is a common reason cartridges wear faster.

Older homes near Historic Downtown may have original valves from decades ago. Parts for those can be hard to find, and replacing the valve may cost less than hunting for a match.
