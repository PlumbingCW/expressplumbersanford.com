Homeowner guide

Do-It-Yourself Water Heater Installation in Sanford, FL

Installing a water heater yourself is possible for a handy homeowner with an electric tank and a simple swap, and it is risky with gas, tight spaces or code upgrades. The job involves water, power or gas and a relief valve that protects your home from scalding and pressure. This guide explains what is involved so you can decide.

By the Sanford Express Plumbing team · Updated November 13, 2026

Call (407) 964-8940

What a water heater installation includes

Draining the old tank, disconnecting water and fuel connections, setting the new unit, connecting water lines, a relief valve with a discharge line and a power or gas connection, then filling, purging air and testing.

Each step has a way to go wrong, and the consequences range from a leak to a gas problem.

Electric versus gas

Electric swaps are simpler because there is no combustion, though the power connection must be correct and the breaker off. Gas adds a gas line, a vent and combustion air, and a mistake can be dangerous.

If you smell gas at any time, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911. Gas work is where most professionals draw the line for homeowners.

The relief valve and discharge line

The temperature and pressure relief valve is a safety device. It opens if temperature or pressure gets too high, and its discharge line must run to a safe place and stay clear.

Capping or plugging a relief valve is dangerous, and a discharge line that is too small or blocked defeats it.

Permits and code

Many jurisdictions require a permit for a water heater replacement, and an inspection afterward. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing sets the base rules, and the local building department enforces them.

Doing the work without a permit can cause trouble with insurance and with a future home sale. Ask before you start.

Tools and time

You need pipe wrenches, a tubing cutter or torch for copper, flexible connectors, dielectric unions, a hose, a bucket, a dolly and a helper. A heater is heavy, and lifting one alone is a way to get hurt.

Plan on most of a day for a first-time job, and expect a trip back to the store.

Where DIY goes wrong

Common mistakes include forgetting to fill the tank before turning on power, which burns out the elements, wrong relief valve discharge, loose connections that drip later and ignoring the expansion tank.

A small leak behind a heater can run for weeks. Our water heater installation page explains what a proper install includes.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber for gas units, attic installs, relocations, fuel changes, tankless conversions and any job where the old connections are corroded. A quick swap after a failure is often best left to a pro.

If your tank is already leaking, water heater replacement explains how we handle it fast.

What a professional adds beyond labor

A plumber carries the right fittings, checks the water pressure, sets an expansion tank when the system needs one and tests the relief valve. We also verify the vent on gas units and measure draft before we leave.

Mistakes that a homeowner may not see, like a slightly backward vent slope or a loose gas fitting, are the ones that cause trouble later. A second pair of experienced eyes is the real value.

A fair way to decide

Ask three questions. Can you lift and place the tank safely, do you know where every shutoff is, and are you comfortable with the fuel the heater uses? If any answer is no, hire the job.

If you do decide to DIY an electric tank, shut off the breaker, test the wires with a tester and fill the tank completely before power goes on. Never skip the permit question.

Safety checks before you decide

Gas heaters add combustion air, venting and gas line risks. A leak or a poor vent can put carbon monoxide in the house, so install a working detector near the bedrooms whatever you decide.

Electric heaters carry a shock risk, and a tank that is not full when power comes on can burn out an element in seconds. If any step feels uncertain, stop and call a plumber.

What the code says about water heaters

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing requires a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge line on a water heater, and manufacturer guidelines set the vent and clearance rules. Water heater replacement is permitted work in most jurisdictions, so ask before you start.

Local note

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Sanford building department what applies to a water heater swap at your address. Lake Mary and Longwood have their own departments.
