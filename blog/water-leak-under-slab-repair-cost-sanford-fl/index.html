Homeowner guide

Water Leak Under Slab Repair Cost for Sanford, FL Homeowners

The cost to repair a water leak under a slab depends on how many leaks there are, where they sit, how old the pipe is and which repair method fits. We do not publish a flat price, because a single leak under a bare slab and several leaks under tile and cabinets are different jobs. This guide explains the factors that set the price and how to compare quotes.

By the Sanford Express Plumbing team · Updated November 13, 2026

Call (407) 964-8940

Locating the leak comes first

Before any repair, the leak must be found. Plumbers use pressure testing, acoustic listening and thermal imaging to pinpoint it, and that work has a cost of its own.

A good locate keeps the opening small. Skipping it and cutting wherever you guess turns a repair into a demolition.

Number and location of leaks

One leak at an accessible spot is the simplest. Several leaks, or a leak under a kitchen island, a bathtub or a load-bearing area, take more work.

If one line has leaked once, others of the same age are likely to follow, so a quote should address the whole line.

The three repair options

A direct repair opens the slab, replaces the pipe section and patches the concrete. A reroute abandons the leaking line and runs a new one through the attic or walls. A repipe replaces the supply lines throughout.

Each has a different price and a different amount of disruption. Our slab leak repair page explains how we choose.

Floor finishes

Tile, wood and carpet over a slab add repair work. Matching tile that was discontinued, or wood that has aged, is often the biggest unknown.

Ask a quote to say what happens to the floor and who handles it. Flooring is usually a separate trade.

Pipe age and material

Older copper pipe that is failing in one place is likely to fail in others, which can tilt the decision toward a reroute or repiping. Newer pipe with a single failure may suit a spot repair.

We tell you plainly when a bigger repair costs less over time than repeated small ones.

Water damage and drying

A slab leak can soak flooring and walls. Drying and mold prevention are separate from the plumbing repair and often handled by a restoration company.

Dry the area quickly. Mold can start in a few days in Florida's humid air.

Insurance and slab leaks

Policies differ. Many cover resulting water damage but not the cost of accessing the leak or repairing the pipe, and some exclude slab leaks or gradual seepage.

Ask your agent before work starts, and keep photos, the plumber's report and receipts.

Comparing quotes

Compare what each quote includes: locating, repair method, patching, permits and warranty. A low number that leaves out the floor is not the same as a higher number that includes it.

Ask how the plumber confirmed the leak and what happens if a second leak is found.

Questions to ask before you hire

Ask how the leak will be located and confirmed, whether the quote includes patching the slab and flooring, and what the warranty covers. Ask who handles the permit.

Ask what happens if a second leak shows up after the repair, and how the plumber will test the system before closing the floor.

What a fair quote should say

A fair quote names the leak location, the method, the materials, the patching and the timeline. It separates the locate from the repair and says what is not included.

If a quote is a single number with no scope, ask for a breakdown. Ask for a price before work starts and for an explanation of every line.

What changes the final price

Depth of the leak, the size of the area opened, the type of flooring above and whether the pipe can be rerouted instead of dug up all move the price. A leak under tile costs more to restore than one under bare slab.

Ask for the locate, the repair and the restoration to be priced as separate lines. That way you can compare quotes fairly and see where the money goes.

Codes that apply to slab repair

According to code guidelines, slab repairs fall under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and the repaired line must pass a pressure test before the floor is closed. Ask your building department whether a permit is needed for the work.

Local note

Slab leaks in Sanford and Seminole County

Most homes in this part of Seminole County are built on concrete slabs, so supply lines often run under the floor. Sandy soil can shift after heavy rain, and that stress can crack older lines.

The City of Sanford's water quality report does not give a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask the City of Sanford Utility Department if you wonder about water quality and pipe wear. The report also lists chlorine as the disinfectant, which is common across Florida.
