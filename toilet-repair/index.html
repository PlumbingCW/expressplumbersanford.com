Overflow

Overflowing toilet emergency: stop the water in seconds

An overflow moves fast, and the right actions in the first minute keep water on the bathroom floor instead of in the ceiling below.

  1. 1
    Close the supply valve. It is behind or beside the toilet and turns clockwise.
  2. 2
    Stop the tank filling. Lift the tank lid and press the flapper closed if the valve sticks.
  3. 3
    Do not flush again. Another flush adds more water to a blocked line.
  4. 4
    Call and ask about help. Tell us whether the overflow is isolated to one toilet or other drains are slow too.
Common problems

Running, leaking, clogged and wobbling toilets

A toilet that runs on has a flapper that does not seal or a fill valve that does not shut off. A toilet that leaks at the base has a failed wax ring or a loose bolt, and a toilet that rocks has a bad flange or an uneven floor.

Clogs usually respond to a plunger or auger. If more than one fixture is affected, the problem is in the drain, and drain cleaning is the better path.

Flange repair

Broken toilet flange and loose bowls

The flange is the ring that anchors the toilet to the drain. When it cracks or rusts, the toilet rocks, the wax seal fails and water seeps under the floor. A repair ring or a full flange replacement stops the movement and seals the connection.

Floors that look fine can hide rot after a long leak. If you see a soft spot or a stain around the base, tell us so we can check the subfloor before reinstalling.

Replace or repair

When to replace a toilet instead of repairing it

A toilet with a cracked bowl or tank, a repeated clog from a poor flush design or a model that wastes water is a candidate for replacement. Parts for much older models can be hard to find, and the cost of repeated repairs can pass the cost of a new toilet.

A new toilet also lets you pick a comfort height, a better flush and a water-efficient model. Installation includes a new wax ring, new supply line and a check of the flange.

Local

Toilets in Sanford homes

Many local homes sit on a concrete slab, so a toilet leak seeps into the floor rather than the ceiling below, and you may not notice it until the flooring lifts or smells. A toilet that wobbles slightly is worth checking before it turns into a bigger repair.

A fast-running toilet can quietly waste water. If your water bill is up, a toilet test is a cheap first check, and the next step is leak detection if the meter still moves.

Rules and references

Efficiency standards for a new toilet

Toilets sold in the United States are limited by federal standards to 1.6 gallons per flush, and WaterSense labeled models use no more than 1.28. According to EPA guidelines, labeled models are tested for performance as well as savings.

Replacing a toilet is a fixture swap and usually needs no permit, but ask your building department if the drain or the flange has to move.
