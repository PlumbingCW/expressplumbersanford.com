Local record

Where Georgetown sits and how it began

A heritage marker catalogued by the University of Central Florida says Henry Shelton Sanford established Georgetown in the 1870s as a settlement for migrating Black families. It lies east of Sanford Avenue and north of Celery Avenue. The first school opened in 1885, and one of its principals, J. N. Crooms, later founded Crooms Academy.

The marker also notes that St. James African Methodist Episcopal Church, designed by the Tuskegee-trained builder Prince W. Spears, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Many early settlers' properties are still held by their second and third generations.

City rules

What the historic district designation changes

The City of Sanford lists a Georgetown Historic District on the National Register and a Georgetown Residential Historic District among its local districts. For Georgetown, the city says a Certificate of Appropriateness is required for demolition and new construction.

The page we read does not mention interior plumbing repairs. Replacing a fixture or clearing a drain is a different kind of work from building or tearing down. If a job might change the outside of a building, call the city's historic preservation staff first, and ask your plumber to hold off on that part until you have an answer.

General information

Plumbing checks that apply to any house

These checks do not depend on the age of the building. They are the questions a plumber starts with when you call.

  • Where is the shutoff? Find your main water valve now, before there is a problem. Our burst pipe repair page shows what to do after it is closed.
  • Is a drain running slowly everywhere? That points to the main line. A camera inspection or drain cleaning is the next step.
  • Do floors or walls feel damp? Leak detection can find the source without opening everything.
  • Planning a rebuild or addition? Talk to the city first, then see plumbing installation for rough-in and fixture planning.
Honest limits

What we have not verified

We have no published record of the pipe material, age or condition of individual Georgetown houses, so this page does not describe any. We also have not confirmed exact street boundaries for the district, which is why we describe it by the Sanford Avenue and Celery Avenue reference from the marker.

For the wider Sanford picture, start from our Sanford plumbing page.

Practical note

Permits and code, in one paragraph

Plumbing work in Florida falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and many jobs need a permit from the local building division. Ask the City of Sanford what applies to your address, and ask your plumber who pulls the permit before work starts.

Sources for the local facts above: University of Central Florida Georgetown Historic Marker record (richesmi.cah.ucf.edu) and the City of Sanford historic preservation page (sanfordfl.gov). Plumbing information is general.
