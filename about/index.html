About us

How we work

We built this business around one idea: when water is on the floor, the first call should reach a person. Call to ask about availability.

The person who answers asks what is happening, tells you what to shut off and asks about the next available plumber for your address. Ask for a price before any work starts, and ask the plumber to talk to you if the job changes.

Scope

What we do and do not do

We handle drains, sewer lines, water heaters, leaks, pipes, fixtures, disposals, repiping and commercial plumbing. The full list is on the all services page.

We do not post flat prices online, because each job is different. We also do not promise a fixed arrival time, since the job before yours and traffic on I-4 are real. We do not offer gas line work through this site.

Trust

Verify any plumber's license with the state

Ask about licensing when you call. You can check any plumber's license yourself with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation at myfloridalicense.com before work begins.

Plumbing in Florida follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and permit requirements are set by the city or county where the work is done. Ask the local building department what applies to your address.

Calls

What happens when you call

The person who answers asks for your street address first, because location decides which plumber can reach you. Next comes a short set of questions about what you see, hear and smell, and whether anyone is in danger.

If water is spreading, you are told how to shut off the main valve or the fixture valve. If a drain is backing up, you are told which fixtures to stop using. Those first steps often limit the damage before help arrives.

A plumber looks at the problem, explains what is wrong and you can ask for a price before work starts. If the job turns out to be larger than first described, the plumber stops and talks to you before going further.

Homes and businesses

Jobs we take

Homeowners call us for clogged drains, backed-up sewer lines, leaking water heaters, running toilets, dripping faucets, jammed disposals and pipes that burst or leak inside a wall. Property managers and shop owners call for restroom backups and water heater failures that cannot wait.

Planned work is welcome too. Water heater replacement, repiping and new fixture installation can be discussed on the same line, and you can ask about scheduling. Read the all services page to see the full list.

Principles

Our standards

We explain a problem in plain words and we do not recommend work that the problem does not need. We would rather tell you that a clog is a ten minute job than sell a bigger repair.

We do not post reviews or ratings on this website, because we only publish claims we can stand behind. Our service pages describe how each job works, what affects the price and when a problem is urgent, so you can judge us before you call.

Ask about availability at night and on weekends when you call. A burst pipe at three in the morning is still a burst pipe, so ask about availability when you call.

How to reach us

Why a phone line and not a form

Plumbing problems do not wait for a reply to an email. A burst pipe, a sewer backup or a water heater that is flooding the garage needs a person on the line who can tell you what to shut off right now.

That is why this website has no contact form and no chat box. Every button goes to the phone. If you prefer to write, you can send a message to our contact page email, though a call is faster for anything urgent.

When you do call, have the street address, the nearest cross street and a short description ready. Tell us whether anyone in the home is elderly, whether pets are inside and whether you can reach the main shutoff. Those details help us send the right plumber with the right equipment on the first trip.

We also keep our guides for homeowners on the blog, covering water heaters, toilets, faucets, drains and leaks, so you can read before you decide whether a problem needs a plumber today.
