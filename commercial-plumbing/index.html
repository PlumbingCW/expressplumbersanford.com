After-hours emergency

Commercial plumbing emergency: stabilize the building first

A burst line, a sewage backup in a restroom or a failed water heater can end a business day. The first minutes decide how much stock, flooring and revenue is lost.

  1. 1
    Close the right valve. Shut the main or the zone that serves the problem area.
  2. 2
    Cut power where water is present. Turn off breakers to wet equipment if you can do it safely.
  3. 3
    Protect stock and records. Move what you can above the waterline.
  4. 4
    Call and ask about help. Tell us the building type, the symptom and whether the sewer or the supply is involved.
Scope

What commercial plumbing covers

Commercial work differs from residential in scale and consequences. Pipes are larger, systems are shared, and failures affect customers and health inspections. We handle repairs, backups, water heaters, restroom fixtures, floor drains, grease line service and troubleshooting.

For a main drain backing up into a kitchen or restroom, see sewer line cleaning and hydro jetting, which are commonly used on grease-heavy commercial lines.

Restaurants

Restaurants, breweries and food service

Kitchens produce grease that cools and sticks to line walls. Floor drains, prep sinks and dishwashers share lines that clog at the worst time, usually on a Friday night.

Grease traps and interceptors need regular pumping on a schedule set by the operator and the local authority. Ask the City of Sanford or your landlord what applies to your site, since requirements differ by jurisdiction.

Older buildings

Historic storefronts and older commercial lines

Many storefronts on and around First Street date to the rebuilding after the 1887 fire, when downtown was rebuilt in brick, according to the City of Sanford. Buildings that age have been renovated many times, and the pipe behind the walls often mixes old and new materials.

That is a practical reason to plan before a remodel. A camera look at the drain and a pressure test on the supply tell you what you are connecting to before the walls are open.

What to do while you arrange help

Steps for a business during a plumbing failure

Close the affected restroom or area, put out wet floor signs and shut the nearest fixture valve or the main. Move stock and electronics away from the water.

Photograph the damage for your records, note the time the problem began and keep staff out of standing water near outlets. If sewage is involved, keep the public out of the area until it is cleaned.
